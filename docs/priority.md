# Priority: leads answer however busy the machine is

A person's message is answered whatever the machine is doing (issue #421). This is how the core keeps it so.

## A lead's turn never waits for strain

Before a turn starts, `Runs.launch` reads Linux's `MemAvailable` and its memory and CPU pressure (`core.Strained`; `mem_floor_mb`, `mem_pressure_max`, `cpu_pressure_max` in `.hive/config.json`) and holds the run while any is past its limit. A lead (the Hive-wide lead and each project's; `Run.Lead`) is held by none of them. Its one hold is an absolute floor of 256 MB available (`core.LeadMemFloorMB`), under which its harness could not run at all; that hold is journalled once as `run.held`, as every hold is, so the control center says why. `load_max` only ever held workers (the work module's queue) and is unchanged. A hold on every run (`Runs.Hold`, as for an update or a test's end) still holds leads: the exemption is from the machine's strain, not from the daemon's own pauses. Only the daemon makes a lead: the `spawn` op starts workers and reviewers only (`TestSpawnNeverMakesALead`), so no agent gives itself a lead's exemption or priority.

## One memory budget across every role

Every agent turn this machine runs counts against one budget (issue #1849: 27 live turns, ~240 MB each, filled the Hive's 10 GB share while the work module's pace said one worker at once, since wardens, reviewers, sweeps, step-backs and reopened or resumed turns were never counted). The core's part is a hook: after the strain hold, `Runs.launch` asks `Runs.Admit` about every turn but a lead's that would start here, under its lock, with the runs whose turns are live or starting here, and holds the run with the reason it gives, journalled as `run.held` once per kind. The policy is the budget module's (`internal/mod/budget`). It lets a turn in only while the live and starting turns, those ahead of it in line and itself, at the per-turn size, fit in the Hive's limit less `mem_floor_mb`, and while the room left less `mem_floor_mb` holds a turn for itself and each ahead. The limit is `mem_budget_mb`, else the smallest `memory.high` or `memory.max` on the daemon's cgroup and those above it, else `MemTotal`. The room is `MemAvailable`, or, under a cgroup's limit, that limit less the cgroup's `memory.current` with its `inactive_file` pages (`memory.stat`) counted back as room, whichever is less: `memory.current` counts page cache, which the Hive's clones and Go builds keep near `memory.high`, and the kernel reclaims it before holding anything. A limit from `mem_budget_mb` is weighed against `MemAvailable` alone. The per-turn size is `turn_mb`, else learned every 30 seconds from the RSS of each live turn's process tree (an average blended 0.7/0.3 into the last), starting at 250 MB. A lead's turn counts but is never asked. The others wait in rank (`budget.Rank`): the merge path's readers and wardens (reviewer, security, warden, tribunal, judge), then workers and the rest, then sweeps, step-backs and the filer, each rank in the order it was first held; a held run not asked about for a minute leaves the line. A step-back waits while another runs, across projects, holding no place meanwhile. At most `turn_burst` (3) turns start in any 20 seconds, leads aside, so neither a lifted hold (40 freed at 03:03Z on 10-28) nor a burst of reopened runs (19 at once at 02:39Z) starts a crowd. The control center's agents row says `turns N of S in memory, K waiting`, and the status op's `turns` line says the same by role.

## Leads step first

`Runs.Tick` steps the leads first, each on its own goroutine, started in `Run.Nice` order (the Hive-wide lead first) but running side by side, so the order is one of priority, not of who finishes first, and steps the other runs only once every lead's step is done, waiting at most 5 seconds for that on a goroutine of its own, never on the daemon loop; a lead's step still going at 5 seconds is named in `daemon.log`, once a stall: a later tick can't claim the hung lead, so its pass has no lead to wait for and goes at once (`TestHungLeadStepIsSaidOnce`). One such pass waits at a time: ticks that come while it waits step their leads and skip the pass, rather than pile up a waiting goroutine each. The other runs are listed after the wait, as they are then, and each is claimed as it is now (`Runs.claim`), so a tick that overlaps a slow lead step never steps a run from an older view. While a lead's launch is in flight (`Runs.leadLaunching`), a launch of any other role returns at once and is due again next tick, so a lead's turn has its process before a queued worker's or review's starts; a run deferred ten ticks in a row is named in `daemon.log`. The order holds at the start of a launch only: a worker's launch already past that check when a lead's becomes due goes ahead beside it.

## Leads run at a higher priority

Every sandboxed command carries a nice value (`sandbox.Spec.Nice`, from `Run.Nice`): the Hive-wide lead 0, a project's lead 2, every other run 10, and checks and the recursive gate (`hive gate`, which the daemon starts itself) 15 (`core.CheckNice`). A process can only lower its own priority, so each level adds to the daemon's own and is capped at 19 (`sandbox.Lowered`). The bare and Seatbelt sandboxes and the gate prefix `nice -n` through `sandbox.Niced`, which resolves both `nice` and the command on the daemon's own PATH, never on the command's (which carries the tools bins an agent can write to); a relative path with a slash (`./x`) is left as it is, for exec to find from the command's own directory, and Seatbelt runs macOS's own `/usr/bin/sandbox-exec`. With no `nice` on the daemon's PATH a command runs at the daemon's level, said once in `daemon.log`. The Linux launcher (`hive sandbox-exec`) reads its own level and sets the sum with `setpriority` on a thread its goroutine is locked to, since Linux nices a thread and the sandbox is forked from that goroutine later; the init, bubblewrap and the command inherit it, under WSL too. A failed `setpriority` doesn't stop the command; it is said in the run's stderr log (`<stdout>.err`). Only the forked sandbox is lowered: goroutines the launcher starts on other threads (the egress server, the signal loop) keep the daemon's level, so host-side work moved into the launcher would too.
