# The judge

A model's judgement decides whether a change is kept; the numbers are one thing it weighs. The person, 2026-10-24: "the scoring needs to be a data point into a judgement call from the model cuz some things might not result in increase in score but will def help". Code: `internal/core/judge.go` (the record, the model pick), `internal/mod/work/judge.go` (the run), `internal/mod/improve/judge.go` (the judge after a merge); the role prompt is `modules/agents/judge.md`.

## Where it decides

- **After a merge** (every project, improve module). When the tests stay green but the score falls past its tolerance, measured against the merge's parent scored beside it in one sitting (docs/score.md, "Time and noise"), after a change taken as a gain (anything not clearly a fix, issue #568), the judge reads the change, its brief and account, and the score with its last 20 measurements on main, and decides whether main keeps the merge or reverts it. A keep records the measurement as kept and moves the baseline to this score, since main now stands there (unless a later merge was measured meanwhile, whose baseline stands). While one merge is with its judge, later merges of the project wait to be measured against the baseline it settles, so the same fall isn't judged twice; a judge run that takes over 3 hours (`work.JudgeWait`) is stopped before the next is asked, and a merge with its judge for over an hour raises a `judge` alert, since later merges wait on it. After a restart, a merge whose judge run is still going holds later merges again (`holdForJudge`, from `Recover`) until it is measured again and finds that run by its id (`TestRestartHoldsLaterMergesForTheJudge`). `numbers.md` says how many falls judges have kept in a row and how far that lowered the baseline from the best before them, so a run of small kept falls can't drift the baseline down unseen. Red tests, a score that stopped producing a number, and a revert that left main worse go as before, unjudged: those are not the score.

A rise in the score after a merge needs no judgement. Nor does a fall after a clear fix (a task whose aim is fix, naming the failure it fixes): the fix is kept, its score is the baseline from there, and improve files a brief to look into the dip, whose worker reverts the fix only on judging that the fix itself made things worse (`TestFixWinsOverAScoreDip`). Once per cause, and only while a worker reads it, not per dip: a dip while an earlier look is new, queued or with its worker is added to that look as context (one past its worker, in review or merge, gets the dip a look of its own), and a dip read after main has moved on files none when main's head, measured then, is back within tolerance of the score before it (`TestNoSecondLookIntoAKnownOrGoneDip`; three of four looks on 2026-10-27 re-found one cause, the holdGap stall #1764 fixed).

## How it runs

The judge is a read-only run of role `judge` on a clean checkout of the change, with `brief.md` (what it decides, what the numbers alone say, the change's brief and account, and a token), `diff.patch` and `numbers.md` in `$HIVE_RUN`. Its answer is structured (`core.JudgeSchema`): `verdict` keep or rollback, `summary`, and the token. The daemon records it (`judge.decided` in the journal) only from a read-only judge run it started on exactly that change (`Effects.RecordJudgement`), and says it on the PR. The run is named by the change (`j-<task>-<merge>-<try>`), so a restart finds it rather than starting another. A judge that gives no usable answer three times (`work.JudgeTries`) leaves the numbers to decide, and the task's log says so. As a check, the judge spends the plan's reserve between 90% and 95% of a login's week (`core.Reserved`); at 95% it parks like every run.

**Security-listed files.** A measured rollback of a change that touches files on `tests/LANDING-security.toml` (or the root class, `Change.Security`) stands as the numbers said, with no judge (`work.Unjudged`, `TestJudgeDoesNotOverrideASecurityRollback`): the judge reads the diff, so a prompt injection in it could otherwise reach the merge. The same holds after a merge: a fall in the score of such a merge reverts unjudged (`TestSecurityFallRevertsUnjudged`). A keep there is still judged, since a judge can only roll it back.

**Its model** (`Config.JudgePick`): a `judge` route the person sets comes first, then the reviewer's model, then the tribunal seats, taking the first that isn't the model the change's worker ran on, so a change isn't judged by its author. When every choice is the author's model it runs there and raises an alert. As a check, it doesn't move along the fallback list at a usage limit unless the person routes it.

Under the fake harness the judge goes with the numbers, unless the change asks `FAKE:judgekeep` or `FAKE:judgerollback`.
