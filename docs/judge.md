# The judge

A model's judgement decides whether a change is kept; the numbers are one thing it weighs. The person, 2026-10-24: "the scoring needs to be a data point into a judgement call from the model cuz some things might not result in increase in score but will def help". Code: `internal/core/judge.go` (the record, the model pick), `internal/mod/work/judge.go` (the run, the gate's judge), `internal/mod/improve/judge.go` (the judge after a merge); the role prompt is `modules/agents/judge.md`.

## Where it decides

- **The recursive gate** (a change to the Hive aimed at a gain, docs/gate.md). Once the gate has measured both sides (`report.json`'s `measured`, `GateResult.Measured`), keep or rollback, the judge reads the change's brief and its worker's account, the diff, and the gate's whole report, and decides. Its keep merges a change the numbers called "not better"; its rollback sends back one the numbers kept. The merge step (`Effects.Merge`, via `Effects.Kept`) takes the judge's newest call on that head, at the main of the verdict that stands (`Effects.StandingGate`: the exact main, or an older one main has since moved past without touching the change or the gate's and score's paths), over the gate's outcome. A candidate that failed before it was measured (it doesn't build, fails its own vet or short tests, rewrote its binary) is rolled back as the gate said, and a gate that couldn't run still waits: there is nothing for a judge to weigh.
- **After a merge** (every project, improve module). When the tests stay green but the score falls past its tolerance after a change taken as a gain (anything not clearly a fix, issue #568), the judge reads the change, its brief and account, and the score with its last 20 measurements on main, and decides whether main keeps the merge or reverts it. A keep records the measurement as kept and moves the baseline to this score, since main now stands there (unless a later merge was measured meanwhile, whose baseline stands). While one merge is with its judge, later merges of the project wait to be measured against the baseline it settles, so the same fall isn't judged twice; a judge run that takes over 3 hours (`work.JudgeWait`) is stopped before the next is asked, and a merge with its judge for over an hour raises a `judge` alert, since later merges wait on it. After a restart, a merge whose judge run is still going holds later merges again (`holdForJudge`, from `Recover`) until it is measured again and finds that run by its id (`TestRestartHoldsLaterMergesForTheJudge`). `numbers.md` says how many falls judges have kept in a row and how far that lowered the baseline from the best before them, so a run of small kept falls can't drift the baseline down unseen. Red tests, a score that stopped producing a number, and a revert that left main worse go as before, unjudged: those are not the score.

One exception skips the gate's judge: when the gate can't judge at main because main itself fails in it (`main_fails`, docs/gate.md), the person may land a held change to the gate's own code from its Waiting item. That "gate" approval, for that exact sha at the main it was asked at, stands in for the judge's keep in `Effects.Merge` as well as the gate's: nothing was measured for a judge to weigh. The judge after the merge still runs if the score falls.

A rise in the score after a merge needs no judgement: the change already passed its reads. Nor does a fall after a clear fix (a task whose aim is fix, naming the failure it fixes): the fix is kept, its score is the baseline from there, and improve files a brief to look into the dip, whose worker reverts the fix only on judging that the fix itself made things worse (`TestFixWinsOverAScoreDip`).

## How it runs

The judge is a read-only run of role `judge` on a clean checkout of the change, with `brief.md` (what it decides, what the numbers alone say, the change's brief and account, and a token), `diff.patch` and `numbers.md` in `$HIVE_RUN`. Its answer is structured (`core.JudgeSchema`): `verdict` keep or rollback, `summary`, and the token. The daemon records it (`judge.decided` in the journal) only from a read-only judge run it started on exactly that change (`Effects.RecordJudgement`), and says it on the PR. The run is named by the change (`j-<task>-<head>-<main>-<try>`, `j-<task>-<merge>-<try>`), so a restart finds it rather than starting another. A judge that gives no usable answer three times (`work.JudgeTries`) leaves the numbers to decide, and the task's log says so; at the gate a judge run past `work.JudgeWait` is stopped and counts as one of those tries, so a hung judge can't hold a task for ever (the stall watcher skips the gate's wait). The numbers decide too for a judge of a gate's clear keep (`clearKeep`: its gain a metric measured beyond the noise, not a pass rate the control's flake could have given it) that has stayed parked for `work.JudgeParkWait` (30 minutes, measured from when it went to parked, `Run.ParkedSince`: at a usage limit, or on a model that can't answer): it is stopped, the keep merges on its numbers, and the PR says "Judge bypassed" so a later read knows no judge weighed it (issue #983: #868's keep waited 8 hours on its judge while main stood still for 12). A rollback, or a keep won on a pass rate, still waits for its judge, which may keep what the numbers missed or roll back a win the control's flake gave. As a check, the judge spends the plan's reserve between 90% and 95% of a login's week (`core.Reserved`); at 95% it parks like every run.

**Security-listed files.** A measured rollback of a change that touches files on `tests/LANDING-security.toml` (or the root class, `Change.Security`) stands as the numbers said, with no judge (`work.Unjudged`, `TestJudgeDoesNotOverrideASecurityRollback`): the judge reads the diff, so a prompt injection in it could otherwise reach the merge. The same holds after a merge: a fall in the score of such a merge reverts unjudged (`TestSecurityFallRevertsUnjudged`). A keep there is still judged, since a judge can only roll it back.

**Its model** (`Config.JudgePick`): a `judge` route the person sets comes first, then the reviewer's model, then the tribunal seats, taking the first that isn't the model the change's worker ran on, so a change isn't judged by its author. When every choice is the author's model it runs there and raises an alert. As a check, it doesn't move along the fallback list at a usage limit unless the person routes it.

**Lessons.** The daemon runs the gate with `--judged`, so the gate writes no lesson for a measured rollback; the judge's rollback writes it, and so does the numbers' rollback when the judge gave no usable answer `JudgeTries` times or wasn't asked (security-listed files), in the gate's own words (never the judge's summary, which read the candidate's diff; `TestJudgeWithNoAnswerLeavesTheLesson`). A measured keep the judge rolled back goes to the worker with the judge's reasons, worded as kept by the numbers and rolled back by its judge, and its lesson says the same.

Under the fake harness the judge goes with the numbers, unless the change asks `FAKE:judgekeep` or `FAKE:judgerollback`.
