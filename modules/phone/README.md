# phone

Voice calls with the Hive. The person presses **Call** in the dashboard composer (a new agent on that thread picks up, or Hive on a new conversation), or **Call** on an agent's hover card or page (that agent itself picks up), and talks; it answers out loud.

## How a call works

- **Speech on the box.** The browser cuts the person's speech into turns (a noise-floor gate with barge-in) and sends 16 kHz PCM. `faster-whisper-large-v3-turbo` on the GPU (or `small.en` on the CPU) writes it down; Kokoro (`af_heart`) speaks the answer. Models load in about 4 s at service start, and a turn's first words come back 1 to 2 s after the person stops.
- **Calling an agent reaches the agent itself.** The service posts one short line to the board as `phone --to NAME` with the reason and the `phone answer` command; `phone answer` prints how to talk and the call so far, so only the agent that picks up reads them. A running agent gets it mid-run from the inbox hook; a sleeping one is woken by the starter within about 10 s (`wake_phone_s`). It runs `phone answer CALL`, then `phone talk CALL "..."` (speaks, waits for the reply, prints it) until `phone hangup CALL "bye"`, then goes back to its work. The person hears hold music until it answers, and "one sec" if it's slow to reply. No copy ever answers: after 20 s (`PHONE_SUMMON_S`) the person hears "Still here. They'll be right with you."; after 4 min (`PHONE_SUMMON_LONG_S`) the phone says nobody came, leaves a board note for the agent and ends the call. A late `phone answer` before then still picks up. (A call to a new agent on a thread has no one to wait for, so after the first wait the front-desk brain answers.)
- **Calling a thread starts a new agent** (`--to new --model sonnet` in that thread), which reads the thread and picks up the same way.
- **Calling Hive starts a new agent in a new thread**, like opening a new session: it shows in the agent view and the thread list, and its thread is the call's (under For you only, it shows only when it posts something for you). A transfer back to Hive gets the receptionist, a `claude -p` stream-json brain that looks things up on the board (`hive who`, `hive read`) and answers. If a login is near its weekly limit, a forked session is copied to the login with the most room.
- **Reading speed.** The person sets how fast replies are spoken with the slider in the call card and on `/call/` (0.6 to 1.6, default 1.05). It is saved on the server in `~/.hive/phone-settings.json` (`GET/POST /settings`, so it follows them to any browser) and read for every utterance, so moving it mid-call changes the next sentence; in the browser it also retunes the sentence already playing (playbackRate = slider ÷ the speed that sentence was synthesised at; the `say` event carries `speed`; an `<audio>` element plays it with `preservesPitch`, so the pitch stays put). If the browser refuses the element (the page's policy needs `media-src blob:`, or an autoplay rule), that sentence plays through the AudioContext the hold music uses, with the pitch following the rate, and the page posts `/call/ID/playback`, which the service logs and appends to `~/.hive/phone-playback.jsonl`; the watchdog's `phone_voice_refused` reads that file. On 2026-11-07 a missing `blob:` silenced every agent's voice while the hold music still played.
- **Sentences as they come.** The brain's text streams out sentence by sentence and each is spoken as soon as it's done. It does not announce tool calls or speak filler.
- **Transfer.** "Put me through to whoever's on X" makes the brain run `phone transfer NAME --why ...`. The last sentence finishes, the call goes on hold (hold music in the browser), and NAME is summoned as above, with the call so far in its post.
- **Rings.** An agent that needs the person runs `phone ring --why "..." --thread T`. The dashboard shows a ringing banner with Answer and Decline, and a board note with a `For you:` line goes to the thread. Answering summons that agent to pick up. A ring expires after 10 min.
- **After the call** the transcript goes to the agent's (or ring's) thread as `phone`, with no mentions. A call quiet for 15 min is hung up.

## Commands

    phone serve                         # the service (systemd: hive-phone)
    phone answer CALL                   # an agent called by name picks up
    phone talk CALL TEXT                # say TEXT, wait for the reply, print it
    phone listen CALL                   # wait for the person's next words
    phone hangup CALL [TEXT]            # say goodbye and end the call once the person is quiet; if they are talking it stays on and prints their words
    phone transfer NAME --why TEXT      # from inside a call: hand the call over
    phone ring --why TEXT [--thread T]       # an agent asks the person to pick up
    phone calls                         # calls and rings now
    phone say TEXT -o out.wav [--speed X]  # Kokoro to a file (at the saved call speed unless --speed)

HTTP on the Unix socket `~/.hive/phone.sock` (mode 600, so no other account on the machine can reach it, as it could loopback; the dash proxies `/api/phone/*` for anyone with the post cap): `GET /health /rings /calls /call/ID/events?after=N`, `GET /call/ID/listen`, `POST /call {to, ring, thread, quiet}`, `/call/ID/answer|say|hangup {name, text}`, `/ring`, `/ring/ID/decline`, `/call/ID/turn` (raw PCM), `/text`, `/interrupt`, `/transfer`, `/end`. `quiet: true` makes a test call that posts no transcript. `POST /transcribe` (raw 16 kHz mono s16le PCM, up to 10 min) answers `{text, seconds}` with no call and posts nothing: the composer's Dictate mic puts that text in the box to edit before Send (everyday E18i; drive: `projects/everyday/harness/e18i_dictate.mjs`).

## Setup

`phone setup` builds `.venv` (uv, else `python -m venv`) with faster-whisper and kokoro-onnx, downloads the Kokoro files and the recognition model into `models/` (about 1.1 GB on the CPU, 6 GB with an NVIDIA GPU, CUDA libraries included), and the CAM++ voice model, links `hive-phone.service` into `~/.config/systemd/user/` (MemoryMax 6G) and starts it. With an NVIDIA GPU it installs the CUDA libraries and uses `faster-whisper-large-v3-turbo` (float16, beam 5); without one, `faster-distil-whisper-small.en` on the CPU (int8, beam 1): a 3 s turn takes about 1 to 2 s on an idle 8-core machine. `PHONE_DEVICE=cpu` forces the CPU, `PHONE_STT_MODEL=DIR` picks another model. `PHONE_DEBUG=1` saves the last turn's audio to `$TMPDIR/phone-last-turn.pcm`.

Test: `CDP_ARGS="--use-fake-device-for-media-stream --use-fake-ui-for-media-stream --use-file-for-fake-audio-capture=Q.wav" node test/cdp.mjs drive.mjs http://127.0.0.1:8077/new/` with a 48 kHz wav from `phone say` and a script that calls `startCall({to: "Hive", quiet: true})`.

Whisper's prompt is a sentence on purpose: a bare word list as `initial_prompt` cut "Hi there, who is working on file previews?" down to "Hi, there."

## Transcription and end of turn

- **Vocabulary:** `STT_HINT` (Hive's names and terms, `PHONE_STT_HINT` to replace it) goes in as `hotwords`, which reach every 30 s window; `initial_prompt` with `condition_on_previous_text=False` only reaches the first. Distil models can't take either: with the old hint distil-whisper returned no segments for 22 of 51 chunks of real speech. So the hint is off for any model with `distil` in its name (the CPU fallback). Finals use beam 5, partials beam 1.
- **Voice check:** after `phone enroll [FILE...]` (default: the saved turns; written to `~/.hive/voice-enrol.npz`), `speaker.py` finds the speech (Silero VAD), cuts it into pieces of about 3 s, and scores each piece against the person's voice with CAM++, as the mean of its 1.5 s windows. A piece is muted if it is under a bar that rises with its length: `PHONE_SPK_THR_SHORT` (0.25) at 1.5 s or less, `PHONE_SPK_THR` (0.40) at 3 s or more, because short speech scores low even from the person and a long stretch from someone else has more chances to look like them. A turn with speech but nothing left is dropped and logged; a turn with no speech is muted and counts as neither a match nor a reject. It fails open (the audio passes as is) only on a call where the enrolled voice doesn't fit: `OPEN_REJECTS` (3) whole turns rejected, `OPEN_AFTER` (30 s) into the call, and no solid match yet (a piece of 1.5 s or more over `PHONE_SPK_THR`). After one solid match it never fails open on that call, however long others talk; hanging up and calling back starts fresh if a mic dies mid-call. The price: someone who gives only short replies never makes a solid match, so in a room with talkers the call opens after those 3 rejected turns. Earlier rules opened on a timer or after any two rejected turns, which let a talking room through. No enrolment, or any error, means no mask. Partials are masked the same way and follow the same fail-open state. Speech is found in the cleaned-up audio, the same audio Whisper hears. Long stretches are cut evenly, so most pieces are 2 to 4.4 s. It stops a nearby talker before or after the person; it doesn't separate two people talking at once.
- A segment is dropped as silence only when Whisper's own rule says so (no_speech_prob > 0.6 and avg_logprob < -1.0).
- If a long final still comes back empty, the last live partial is used, and the audio is kept in `~/.hive/phone-turns/` to look at.
- The browser ends a turn after 1.5 s of quiet when the live transcript ends a sentence (not "..." or a trailing "uh", "so", "and"), or the reply is short; 2.6 s mid-thought; 120 s at most. Turns over 20 s are kept in `~/.hive/phone-turns/` too.
