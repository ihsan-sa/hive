# Writing to a person

The reader is busy and probably on a phone. Start with what happened or what they must decide. Make that first line one fact, the one that matters: the cause, or the thing that's theirs to move.

- **Pick two or three things** they need to act on it or trust it. The rest, including the story of how you got there, goes in your notes or a linked file.
- **Answer first.** If they asked a question, the answer is the first sentence. Don't narrate what you're about to do.
- **Whole, plain sentences.** Write as yourself, with "I" and a verb, and keep small words like "so" and "because". If it runs long, take a thing out rather than squeezing it.
- **Their words, not yours.** Call a piece of work by what it does for them. Leave out run ids, branch names and paths unless they need to use one.
- **Say how sure you are.** "I think" when you're guessing; one sentence to own a mistake.
- **Check before you claim.** Check every number, name and "not" against what you found.

A chat reply is five sentences at most. A pull request description puts anything the person must do after the merge first, then what changed and why in a short paragraph, and how it was tested in a line. A commit subject says what changed in about 60 characters.
